# APH Findings for the Prototype: New Institution (Initial Approval)

## 1. Scope and source status

This document records the New Institution / Initial Approval findings in the Gemini-generated analysis of the **AICTE Approval Process Handbook (APH) 2024–2027**. It is a project reference describing the reported regulatory requirements, relevant evidence documents, fields, and possible validation checks for one application type.

The findings below are **not an independently verified legal interpretation** of the APH. Before treating a clause, threshold, formula, document requirement, or exception as binding application logic, it should be checked against the cited passage in the original APH PDF. Where the source analysis proposes a technical mechanism rather than quoting a regulatory requirement, it is identified as a platform-level check.

The prototype scope here is **one complete application path: New Institution (Initial Approval)**. Extension of Approval and Change/Expansion are outside this scope.

---

## 2. Application overview

A New Institution application concerns an eligible sponsoring body seeking initial approval to establish an AICTE-regulated technical institution. The Gemini analysis identifies these main areas of evidence:

- Sponsoring-body registration and governance
- Proposed institution name
- Land title, lease conditions, and land use
- Approved building plans and room/carpet-area schedules
- Occupancy/completion status
- Structural stability, where applicable
- Fire safety
- Liquid operational funds
- General AICTE undertaking
- Site location and plot arrangement

The analysis also identifies infrastructure and statutory requirements that depend on the programme, proposed intake, building age, and the nature of the land/building arrangement.

## 4. Fields identified for document extraction

The following are the specific fields reported in the Gemini analysis for the New Institution evidence set. A field being listed here means that the analysis identified it as useful to extract; it does not, by itself, establish that the APH requires the field to appear in every document.

### 4.1 DOC-01 — Applicant registration and governing document

The analysis identifies the registration deed/certificate and governing document (MoA, rules, or equivalent) as evidence of the applicant body's legal identity and constitution.

**Fields of interest**
- Applicant legal name
- Legal entity type (for example, Society, Trust, or Section 8 Company)
- Registration number
- Registration date
- Registering authority
- Registered address
- Relevant objects/purpose clauses
- Non-profit or permitted-activity clauses, where present
- Names and designations of authorised office-bearers/signatories, where shown

**Source references reported:** Clause 1.3.4; Annexure 1.1(viii, x); PDF pp. 2, 77.

### 4.2 DOC-02 — Organisation resolution (Format 3)

**Fields of interest**
- Name of the sponsoring body

## 5. Deterministic checks identified for the New Institution path

These are checks described by the Gemini analysis as computable from extracted evidence and application data. Regulatory outcomes and exact formulas must be verified against the original APH before implementation.

### 5.1 Proposed institution name

**Reported source:** Chapter I, Clause 1.5.4, PDF p. 5.

The analysis identifies a check for protected or prohibited words/acronyms in a proposed institution name. The proposed name and applicant/promoter category are inputs. The reported examples include acronyms such as IIT, IIM, IISc, NIT, IISER, IIIT, IIEST, AICTE, UGC, MoE, and GoI, and protected terms such as Government, India, Indian, National, All India, and Commission.

**Result type:** A potential name-rule finding that records the exact matched term and source provision. The applicability and exceptions depend on the APH wording and applicant category; a string match alone is not proof of ineligibility.

### 5.2 Liquid operational funds

**Reported source:** Chapter I, Clause 1.5.3 and Table 1.2, PDF p. 5.

The analysis reports these minimum amounts:
- ₹100 lakh for Engineering & Technology
- ₹50 lakh for other technical programmes

The check compares the relevant declared, unencumbered funds against the applicable amount. The analysis also notes an exception for Government or State University applicants, for whom minimum budget-allocation evidence may apply in place of bank FDR evidence.

**Inputs:** Proposed programme category; extracted bank certificate amounts; account/FDR holder; stated lien/encumbrance status; applicant category.

**Result type:** Sufficient evidence, apparent shortfall, or review required because the amount, holder, encumbrance status, applicant category, or exception is unclear.

### 5.3 Land tenure and remaining lease period

**Reported sources:** Clause 1.5.1(a); Clause 6.17(a, b); Annexure 1.1; PDF pp. 4, 56, 77.

The analysis reports a 30-year minimum registered lease tenure for relevant lease arrangements and a minimum five-year remaining live lease period for a New Institution application. The deed type determines whether lease-specific checks apply.

**Inputs:** Deed type; registration date/reference; lease commencement/tenure/expiry dates; application submission date; relevant lease affidavit and extension evidence, if applicable.

**Result type:** Meets the reported tenure/remaining-period criteria, apparent shortfall, or manual review where dates/terms are absent or ambiguous.

### 5.4 Number and arrangement of land parcels

**Reported source:** Clause 6.17(d); Annexure 3.1(g, h); PDF pp. 56, 86.

The analysis reports that the site may comprise no more than three plots, with no more than 2 km between plots. It also reports that academic/instructional, administrative, and amenities areas must be on the primary plot, while secondary plots may be used for specified purposes such as sports, hostels, or staff quarters.

**Inputs:** Land parcel identifiers and locations; map coordinates; plan/site allocation of buildings and rooms.

**Result type:** Parcel-count/distance/allocation finding. Reliable distance calculation requires usable coordinates or source-supported measurements; ambiguous plot boundaries or allocations require review.

### 5.5 Building occupancy/completion evidence

**Reported sources:** Clause 6.17(g); Annexure 1.1(iv); PDF pp. 56, 77.

The analysis identifies occupancy/completion/building-licence evidence as relevant and notes an ambiguity around provisional or partial occupancy documentation. It recommends treating provisional status as a review item rather than resolving the ambiguity automatically.

**Inputs:** Document type; issuing authority; issue date; site and building coverage; permanent/provisional/partial status; conditions.

**Result type:** Evidence present and apparently applicable, evidence missing/incomplete, or manual review required. Acceptability of provisional documentation depends on the applicable APH provision.

### 5.6 Structural stability

**Reported sources:** Clause 6.17(h); Annexure 3.1(e); PDF pp. 56, 86.

The analysis reports a Structural Stability Certificate requirement for buildings more than 30 years old, with a five-year certificate validity period and a stated qualification expectation for the structural engineer.

**Inputs:** Building age evidence; relevant building/completion date; certificate issue/expiry or validity; engineer qualification as stated.

**Result type:** Certificate appears applicable and current, certificate apparently absent/out of date, or review required when building age, validity, or qualification cannot be established. The exact age trigger and qualification requirement need source verification.

### 5.7 Fire safety validity and coverage

**Reported sources:** Clause 6.17(i); Annexure 1.1(v); PDF pp. 56, 77.

The analysis identifies a valid Fire Safety Certificate covering the institution's relevant site/buildings as a requirement.

**Inputs:** Institution name; site address; covered blocks/floors; issue date; expiry date or stated validity period.

**Result type:** Apparently current and appropriately scoped, apparently expired, mismatch in named site/institution, or unable to establish validity because the date/term is absent or illegible.

A name/address similarity score can be used as a technical aid, but a similarity threshold is a platform choice, not an APH rule unless the handbook explicitly says otherwise.

### 5.8 Building-plan and infrastructure areas

**Reported sources:** Clause 1.5.1(c); Clause 6.17–6.18; Annexure 3.2, 3.3, 3.4; PDF pp. 4, 56–57, 87–91.

The analysis identifies the approved building plan and room/carpet-area schedule as evidence for assessing the proposed institution's infrastructure. It reports the following example checks for Engineering & Technology, subject to programme and intake applicability:
- Classroom count and minimum classroom carpet area based on divisions, course duration, and division size
- Tutorial-room provision
- First-year and higher-year laboratory provision
- Additional workshop/laboratory requirements for certain disciplines
- Administrative and amenities area baselines
- Circulation-area calculations

The Gemini analysis gives example figures including 66 m² for a classroom serving a division of 60 and 33 m² for a division of 30; four first-year laboratories for total intake up to 600; additional laboratory requirements by course/duration/intake; and, for intake up to 300, administrative area of at least 750 m² and amenities area of at least 500 m².

**Inputs:** Proposed programme/discipline; academic level; proposed intake and division size; course duration; room schedule and areas; carpet-area summary; approved plan.

**Result type:** Calculated apparent sufficiency/shortfall, not assessable due to missing schedule data, or manual review where the programme-specific table/formula or plan interpretation is unclear.

These figures and formulas are particularly important to verify directly in the original APH before they are treated as authoritative requirements.

## 6. Cross-document consistency and document-integrity observations

The Gemini analysis describes several additional platform checks. These are not all regulatory requirements in themselves.

| Check | Evidence/data used | Meaning of a finding |
|---|---|---|
| Identical file uploaded in multiple document slots | File-content hash | Same binary payload appears in more than one slot; reviewer can be shown both locations |
| Sponsoring-body name consistency | Registration document, land deed, bank certificate, affidavits, resolution | Names differ or the relationship between entities is unclear |
| Institution/site address consistency | Land deed, land-use evidence, building plan, occupancy and fire certificates, site map | Address or site descriptions differ and need reconciliation |
| Internal document inconsistency | Header, body, tables, and other pages of one document | Conflicting text within a document requires scrutiny |
| Possible visual alteration | Page image/layout artifacts | Potential anomaly for human examination, not a finding of forgery |
| Digital-signature validity | Signature data and certificate chain, where technically available | Signature verification result or inability to verify |
| QR/official-source verification | QR payload and an available authorized issuer source | Confirmed, pending, or unavailable; lack of access alone does not invalidate the document |
| Plan versus room schedule | Room tags on drawings and schedule entries | Apparent disagreement requiring drawing/schedule review |

The analysis emphasizes that physical readiness—such as whether laboratories contain working equipment or whether facilities are operational—cannot be established from document extraction alone and may require an expert/physical inspection.

## 7. Evidence and finding records

The analysis proposes evidence-linked findings so a reviewer can see the source of a result. A finding can include:

- Finding ID and rule/check ID
- Name of the check
- APH chapter/clause/annexure and page reference
- Status and severity, where defined by the platform
- Plain-language summary
- Source document ID and document type
- Page number
- Field name and extracted value
- Short supporting text or evidence reference
- Explanation of the discrepancy or uncertainty
- Suggested review/resolution route

The Gemini analysis lists these example statuses:

`PASS`, `FAIL_DEFICIENCY`, `EXTRACTION_UNCERTAIN`, `EVIDENCE_INCOMPLETE`, `MISMATCH_DETECTED`, `EXPIRED`, `MANUAL_REVIEW_REQUIRED`, `BLOCKED_BY_PREREQUISITE`.

These are proposed application statuses, not APH terminology unless confirmed in the source.

### Extraction state versus compliance result

An absent or illegible field is an extraction/evidence issue; it is not automatically proof that the underlying requirement is violated. Similarly, a detected inconsistency or visual anomaly is a reason for review, not proof of fabrication. Compliance findings depend on the applicable rule, the evidence available, and any relevant exception.

## 8. Items needing direct APH verification

The Gemini analysis itself records ambiguities and technical boundaries. Important examples for the New Institution path include:

1. **Local FAR/FSI requirements:** the analysis reports that land/building assessment depends on prevailing local norms. A universal FAR/FSI threshold cannot be inferred from the reported material alone.
2. **Provisional/partial occupancy:** the analysis notes potentially differing provisions about provisional occupancy and recommends scrutiny rather than an automatic decision.
3. **Programme-specific infrastructure calculations:** classroom, laboratory, administrative, and amenities calculations depend on the exact applicable APH tables, programme, academic level, intake, and other conditions.
4. **Applicant-category exceptions:** Government/State University or other categories may have different evidence requirements; applicability must be established from the original APH.
5. **External verification:** availability of public APIs for land-registration, fire-safety, or other issuing authorities cannot be assumed. An unavailable source should be distinguished from a failed verification.
6. **Document and signature requirements:** the exact affidavit execution, e-stamp, digital-signature, and document-format conditions require checking against the original provisions.
7. **Technical thresholds:** fuzzy-name/address similarity thresholds, image-anomaly detection, and file hashing are platform mechanisms, not regulatory requirements in themselves.

## 9. Summary

For the selected prototype path, the Gemini analysis describes an initial-approval evidence set centered on applicant identity/governance, land rights and land use, approved buildings and room schedules, occupancy/completion, conditional structural stability, fire safety, operational funds, undertakings, and site mapping.

Document authenticity, ambiguous legal interpretation, physical readiness, and uncertain or unavailable external verification remain matters for scrutiny rather than automatic approval or rejection.
